Summarizing articles

Articles — news stories, blog posts, research pieces — are where most people start. The goal is to go from “too long to read” to “I know what matters” in under a minute.

Step by step

  1. Copy the article text. Paste it into your AI tool. If it is behind a paywall or very long, copy the visible body text only.
  2. Strip anything private. Replace names, IDs, dollar amounts, and addresses with placeholders (see Safety chapter).
  3. Choose length and tone. Decide how short and how formal you want the summary (see Length & Tone chapter).
  4. Paste a prompt. Use one of the templates below, or write your own.
  5. Review the output. AI can hallucinate details. Check names, dates, and numbers against the original.

Prompt templates for articles

Short “TL;DR” (one paragraph):

Summarize this article in one short paragraph. Give me the main point and why it matters, in plain English.

Bullet-point summary (skimmable):

Summarize this article as 5 short bullet points. Cover: what happened, who it affects, and what comes next. Plain English, no jargon.

[PASTE ARTICLE TEXT]

“Explain like I’m 12”:

Explain this article like I am 12 years old. Use simple words, short sentences, and one real-world analogy. No jargon.

[PASTE ARTICLE TEXT]

Professional / executive:

Summarize this article for a busy executive. Include: headline takeaway, key supporting facts, implications, and recommended next action. Professional tone, max 200 words.

[PASTE ARTICLE TEXT]

What to watch for

  • Long articles: Some free tools cut off after a few thousand words. Paste in chunks if needed, or use a local model with a bigger context window.
  • Paywalled content: Only summarize text you have the right to read and share.
  • Dates and stats: AI sometimes invents numbers. Verify any statistic before you repeat it.
